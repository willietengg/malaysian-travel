# China 🇨🇳 (CN · CNY)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- **Mutual visa exemption** in force since 17 Jul 2025: up to **30 days** per visit, max 90 days in any 180 days, for tourism, family, business and exchange. Reported extended for 5 more years (verify the expiry).
- Passport should have 6+ months' validity. Check for any arrival-card or online declaration requirement before departure (verify).

## Money & apps (the most important prep)
- **Alipay and WeChat Pay**: link a Malaysian Visa or Mastercard before you go. Almost everything is paid by QR. Carry some cash as a backup.
- **Google, WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook are blocked on Chinese networks.** Malaysian roaming and many travel eSIMs route data outside China, so these apps keep working. Confirm with the provider before buying.
- Maps: **Amap (高德)** or Baidu Maps. Trains: **12306** (has English) or Trip.com.
- Tax refund for foreign shoppers at participating stores; some cities offer instant refunds (verify the current minimum spend).

## Plug & power
Type A/C/I (I = angled flat pins), 220V. Bring a universal adapter.

## Getting around
High-speed rail is fast and punctual (e.g. Shanghai ↔ Hangzhou ~1h, Guangzhou ↔ Shenzhen ~30 min). Book in advance, with your passport as ID. DiDi for taxis.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Forbidden City closes Monday** (except some holidays) and needs **advance real-name booking with your passport**. The National Museum also closes Monday and needs booking.
- Many top attractions need online booking days ahead. Mainland holiday crowds are extreme.
- **Avoid Golden Weeks** (1–5 May, 1–7 Oct) and the Spring Festival / Chunyun travel rush. Trains sell out and prices spike.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Mixue, Chagee, Luckin, Pop Mart, Miniso and Haidilao are all in MY, so `skip` or `maybe`, unless it's a China-only flagship, menu or limited edition.
- Prices for local food and transport are often lower than in MY. Attractions can be pricey.
- Mandarin speakers will find it easy. Otherwise rely on a translation app (Google Translate offline pack, or Baidu Translate).
