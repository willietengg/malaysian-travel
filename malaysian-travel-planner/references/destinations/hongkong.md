# Hong Kong 🇭🇰 + Macau 🇲🇴 (HK · HKD / MOP)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- **Hong Kong:** visa-free, up to **90 days** (Discover Hong Kong list; the reciprocal deal is the reason Malaysia gives HK passports 90 days). Passport validity: 6+ months is the safe rule (verify on immd.gov.hk).
- **No arrival card**: Hong Kong scrapped arrival/departure cards for visitors (Oct 2024). Frequent visitors can enrol for e-Channel (verify eligibility).
- **Macau:** visa-free, typically **30 days** (verify with the Macao Public Security Police). HK ↔ Macau is a border: you clear immigration on both sides, so carry your passport.
- Going on to Zhuhai/Shenzhen means entering mainland China. Malaysians are visa-free there too (see china.md).

## Money
- **HKD** in HK; **MOP (pataca)** in Macau. Macau shops and casinos widely take HKD at about 1:1, but change often comes back in MOP, and MOP is **not** accepted in HK. Spend it before you leave.
- **Octopus card** for MTR, buses, ferries, 7-Eleven and many eateries. Options: the physical Tourist Octopus, or **Octopus on iPhone / Apple Watch** via the *Octopus for Tourists* app (HK$50 deposit, min HK$100 first load, top up with non-HK Visa / Mastercard / UnionPay in Apple Pay; **no Amex**). Android: limited (verify).
- The MTR has added contactless bank card / mobile wallet entry at gates (verify full coverage).
- **DuitNow QR overseas via Alipay+** (launched 28 Oct 2026): scan merchant QR codes with the Alipay+ logo from the Maybank, CIMB or Hong Leong app; Bank Islam soon. AlipayHK / WeChat Pay HK QR codes are everywhere. There is no direct DuitNow ↔ FPS link.
- No sales tax in HK, so there's **no tourist tax refund**. Prices are what you see.

## Plug & power
HK: Type G (three square pins), 220V. **Same as Malaysia.** Macau: mostly Type G, but some older hotels use round-pin sockets. Bring a universal adapter for Macau (verify).

## Getting around
- MTR + Airport Express, trams ("ding ding"), Star Ferry, minibuses (Octopus or cash). Uber and HKTaxi app. Citymapper and Google Maps work well.
- **To Macau:**
  - **Ferry** (TurboJET / Cotai Water Jet) from Sheung Wan or TST, ~1h. Fares rose ~10% from 25 Apr 2026 (TurboJET economy about HK$194 weekday / HK$212 weekend & holiday / HK$242 night, verify).
  - **HZMB shuttle bus** ("golden bus", 24h) from the HK Port near Tung Chung/airport, ~40 min across the bridge. About HK$65 day / HK$70 night (verify). Cheapest, and convenient if you start from the airport or Lantau (Disneyland).
- In Macau, **free hotel/casino shuttles** link the ferry terminals, border gate and Cotai. There are also public buses and the Macau LRT.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Museums:** **M+ closed Mondays**. **HK Palace Museum closed Tuesdays** (except public holidays) and the first 2 days of Lunar New Year. Government (LCSD) museums each take one weekday off, often Tue or Thu (verify per museum). Many Macau museums close Mondays (verify).
- **Lunar New Year 2027** (6–9 Feb; the 9th is a substitute holiday): many family-run restaurants and small shops close for the first 2–3 days. Fireworks and parades are on, and crowds are big.
- **Mainland Golden Weeks** (1–5 May, 1–7 Oct): Disneyland, Ocean Park, TST and all of Macau get mobbed and hotel prices jump. Avoid them.
- **Typhoon Signal No. 8 / Black Rainstorm** (mainly Jun–Sep): MTR runs a reduced service, shops shut, ferries and often HZMB buses stop, and Disneyland may close. Keep a buffer day and get travel insurance.
- **Hong Kong Disneyland:** dated tickets with tiered prices by date (verify on the official site). *World of Frozen* rides draw the longest queues. Consider Early Park Entry or a Premier Access bundle that includes Frozen (verify which tier). Christmas event runs to 3 Jan 2027.
- **Macau Grand Prix** (19–22 Nov 2026; mid/late Nov yearly): roads near the Guia Circuit close and Macau hotel prices spike.

## Must-eat (diet notes)
| Dish | Where | Diet note |
|---|---|---|
| Dim sum | Everywhere | Siu mai, char siu bao and many others are pork. Halal dim sum exists at certified restaurants |
| Roast goose / char siu / siu yuk | Roast shops | Goose ≠ pork, but chopped on shared boards with pork; not halal |
| Wonton noodles | Central, Mong Kok | Wontons are usually pork + shrimp; broth often pork/fish |
| Egg tarts (HK) / Portuguese egg tarts (Macau) | Bakeries; Lord Stow's (Coloane) | Some pastry uses lard; ask |
| Pineapple bun, milk tea, French toast | Cha chaan teng | Usually no pork, but kitchens are mixed |
| Pork chop bun (Macau) | Taipa | Pork |
| Curry fish balls, egg waffles | Street stalls | Fish / vegetarian-friendly; not certified |
| African chicken, Macanese minchi | Macau | Minchi is often pork; ask |

Halal: certification is by the **Incorporated Trustees of the Islamic Community Fund of Hong Kong** (list covers HK and Macau; certified places may show the Q-Halal mark). Hubs: **Kowloon Mosque & Islamic Centre (105 Nathan Road, TST)**, with TST/Jordan having the most halal restaurants; the Islamic Centre canteen in Wan Chai. See the Discover Hong Kong Muslim travel guide and its prayer-room list.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Cantonese-speaking Malaysians feel at home; English is fine in tourist areas. Unlike the mainland, Google, WhatsApp and Malaysian apps work normally.
- Many HK chains are already in MY (Chow Tai Fook, Giordano, Watsons, Tim Ho Wan, etc.; verify each). Worth it: Mannings and HK pharmacies, Mong Kok sneakers, Disney merch, Koi Kei / Macau almond cookies and jerky (jerky is often pork), local bakeries.
- HK winter (Dec–Feb) can drop to ~10–15°C. Bring a light jacket. Summer is hot, humid and stormy.
- HK is compact: 3–4 days + 1 day (or overnight) in Macau suits most first trips.
