# Australia 🇦🇺 (AU · AUD)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- **ETA (subclass 601)** is required, and Malaysia is eligible. You can only apply through the official **Australian ETA app** (passport chip scan + selfie). There is no visa charge, only an **AUD 20 service fee**. It's valid 12 months (or until the passport expires), multiple entries, up to 3 months per visit, and no work. A new passport needs a new ETA. Avoid lookalike websites that charge AUD 100+.
- **Arrival card:** most airports still use the paper **Incoming Passenger Card**, handed out on the plane. A digital **Australia Travel Declaration** is live on Qantas flights and at Western Sydney International (from 26 Oct 2026), and is rolling out to other airports over 2027–28 (verify for your airport).
- **Biosecurity is very strict. Declare ALL food.** Rendang, serunding, meat floss, sambal with prawns/ikan bilis, salted-egg snacks, egg-yolk mooncakes, fresh fruit, seeds and herbal products all go on the card. Declared items are inspected and usually returned or binned without a penalty. Undeclared items can mean an on-the-spot fine of about AUD 4,000+ (verify current amount) and possibly visa cancellation.

## Money
- AUD ≈ RM2.86 (early Nov 2026). Cards and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, and some cafés are card-only. Small card surcharges are common (the rules are being reformed, verify), and some places add a weekend or public-holiday surcharge.
- Tipping isn't expected.
- **Tourist Refund Scheme (TRS):** get the 10% GST back on purchases of **AUD 300+ from one business (same ABN)** bought within 60 days before departure. You need original tax invoices, and the goods go in your carry-on or are shown at the TRS desk. Pre-fill the claim in the TRS app, then claim at the airside TRS desk after passport control. Leave 30+ min because queues are long.

## Plug & power
Type I (angled flat pins), 230V. Malaysian Type G plugs need an adapter, but no voltage converter.

## Getting around
- **Driving on the left, same as Malaysia.** A Malaysian licence in English is accepted for visitors, but carry a JPJ International Driving Permit too (verify each state and your rental company). Distances are huge. Look out for kangaroos at dusk, fixed speed cameras and toll roads (use the rental company's e-tag).
- **Transport:** Sydney: Opal network, or tap a credit card or phone. Melbourne: myki, and contactless bank cards work on trains since 2026 but trams may still need myki (verify). Brisbane/SEQ: 50-cent flat fares. Perth: SmartRider.
- **Apps:** Google Maps, Uber/DiDi, the official transport app in each state.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Seasons are reversed.** Dec–Feb is hot summer with bushfire and heat risk. Jun–Aug is winter: Melbourne and Canberra get to single digits, and there's snow in the Alps. In the tropical north (Cairns, Darwin) the wet and cyclone season runs Nov–Apr.
- **Christmas Day and Good Friday:** most shops close. **Anzac Day (25 Apr):** shops are closed or open late in many states. Boxing Day sales are huge.
- Big crowds and high prices: school summer holidays (mid-Dec to late Jan), Sydney New Year's Eve, the **Australian Open (11–31 Jan 2027, Melbourne)**, **Vivid Sydney (28 May–19 Jun 2027)** and Easter/school breaks.
- Small-town cafés often close by 15:00, and some restaurants close Mon–Tue.

Halal: Sydney (Lakemba, Auburn), Melbourne (Sydney Rd Brunswick–Coburg), Perth (Northbridge, Mirrabooka). Gold Coast is thinner. Look for AFIC or other recognised certification (verify JAKIM recognition of the certifier if it matters to you). There are many Malaysian-run restaurants in Melbourne and Sydney.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Cotton On and Typo are already in MY, so skip them. Worth buying: Chemist Warehouse vitamins and skincare (check customs limits when bringing them home), UGG (buy the Australian-made ones), Aesop (also sold in MY, so compare prices), Tim Tam flavours not sold in MY.
- Malaysian school holidays (late Nov–Dec) overlap with the Australian summer peak. Book early, or go Mar–May for cheaper fares and mild weather.
