# Indonesia 🇮🇩 (ID · IDR)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

Focus: Bali, Jakarta, Bandung, Yogyakarta.

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- **Visa-free (ASEAN exemption) for up to 30 days, tourism only.** Most sources say it **cannot be extended**. For a longer stay, buy the Visa on Arrival instead (extendable once; verify current fee). Passport valid 6+ months; emergency travel documents are not accepted (verify on imigrasi.go.id).
- **All Indonesia** arrival declaration (allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id or the All Indonesia app): one form covering immigration, customs and health. It **replaced the old e-CD customs form and the SATUSEHAT health form** (from Sep 2025, now nationwide). Fill it in within 3 days before arrival. It's free and shows a QR code. It is not a visa.
- **Bali tourist levy: IDR 150,000 per person** (foreign passports, children included; verify the age rule). Pay on the official **Love Bali** site or app only. Copycat sites charge up to 3x. Pay before you fly; the QR is checked on arrival. Whether it's per entry or per trip: (verify).
- Bali conduct rules: modest dress at temples (sarong + sash), no climbing sacred sites. Renting a motorbike requires an **International Driving Permit** with a motorcycle class. Enforcement got stricter in 2026.

## Money
- IDR has lots of zeros: double-check every amount before you pay. Cash is still needed at warungs, small shops, temples and parking. ATMs charge per withdrawal; use bank ATMs (BCA, Mandiri, BNI) inside malls.
- **Pay with Malaysian bank apps:** DuitNow ↔ **QRIS** cross-border QR has been live since May 2023. Scan any QRIS code at participating merchants. At launch: Maybank, Public Bank, Hong Leong, Touch 'n Go eWallet. More have joined since; check the user's app.
- **VAT refund for tourists:** official rule (DGT) is at least Rp50,000 VAT per receipt and **Rp500,000 VAT in total** (roughly Rp5 million of spending). Only at participating shops; goods bought within 1 month before departure; claim at selected international airports (verify the current airport list and thresholds).
- Multi-currency cards (Wise, BigPay, etc.) avoid bad FX. Avoid street money-changers in Kuta/Legian that offer "no commission" rates.

## Plug & power
Type C / F (two round pins), 230V. **Malaysian Type G plugs need an adapter.** Voltage is fine for MY appliances.

## Getting around
- **Grab** and **Gojek** (cars and bikes) everywhere; Bluebird taxis are reliable in Jakarta and Bali. Some Bali areas have local-transport "no ride-hailing pickup" zones (verify on the spot).
- **Bali:** almost no public transport. Hire a car + driver by the day. Canggu–Seminyak–Ubud traffic is slow, so plan 1–2 areas per day.
- **Jakarta:** MRT, KRL commuter rail and TransJakarta buses; traffic is heavy at peak hours.
- **Jakarta → Bandung:** **Whoosh** high-speed rail, Halim → Tegalluar or Padalarang (~45 min; dynamic fares, verify on the KCIC / Whoosh app). Jakarta → Yogyakarta: KAI trains (book on the KAI Access app or Traveloka) or a ~1h flight.
- Apps: Google Maps works; Traveloka / tiket.com for trains, hotels and attractions.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Nyepi (Bali Day of Silence): Mon 8 Mar 2027.** Whole island shuts ~06:00 8 Mar to 06:00 9 Mar: **Ngurah Rai airport stops commercial flights**, no going out, hotels only, minimal lights. Ogoh-ogoh parades the evening before (7 Mar). Exact 2027 airport schedule: (verify).
- **Ramadan + Lebaran 2027:** Ramadan ≈ Feb–early Mar; **Idul Fitri 10–11 Mar 2027**, with joint leave 9, 12 and 15 Mar. The mudik exodus fills flights, trains and toll roads, and many Java warungs and shops close for several days. Note that Nyepi and Lebaran fall in the same week in 2027.
- **Friday midday:** some Muslim-run shops in Java close around Friday prayers.
- **Borobudur:** climbing the upper structure needs a timed, quota-limited ticket. Reports differ on whether climbing is closed Mondays (verify). **Waisak (20 May 2027)** brings ceremonies and big crowds. **Prambanan:** inner courtyard reportedly closed Mondays (verify).
- Bali **Galungan / Kuningan** (Hindu holidays): some shops close and temples are busy (verify dates).

## Must-eat (diet notes)
| Dish | Where | Diet note |
|---|---|---|
| Nasi padang, rendang | Everywhere | Usually Muslim-run (halal) |
| Gudeg (jackfruit stew) | Yogyakarta | Usually halal; vegetarian versions exist |
| Sate ayam / kambing, bakso | Java | Usually halal in Java; check in Bali |
| Batagor, siomay, seblak | Bandung | Fish-based / vegetarian-friendly; mostly halal |
| **Babi guling** | Bali | **Pork. Skip for halal / no-pork** |
| Ayam betutu, bebek goreng | Bali | Chicken/duck; choose Muslim-run warungs if halal matters |
| Nasi campur Bali, sate lilit | Bali | May contain pork; ask first |
| Martabak manis, es teler | Everywhere | Vegetarian-friendly |

Halal: Java is Muslim-majority, so halal food is easy and musholla prayer rooms are in every mall. **Bali is Hindu-majority**: pork is common, so look for "halal" signs, Padang or Javanese warungs, and the mosques around Kuta/Denpasar. Indonesia's official logo is BPJPH **"Halal Indonesia"** (purple), which replaced the old MUI logo. Mandatory certification phases in, with the small-business deadline in Oct 2026.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Bahasa Indonesia is close to Malay, but watch for false friends (e.g. *butuh*, *percuma*, *kereta* = train in some contexts). Locals usually understand slow Malay.
- Already in MY, so lower priority: J.CO, Mixue, Kopi Kenangan, Chatime (verify). Worth it: Bandung factory outlets and local fashion labels, Yogyakarta batik and silverwork (Kotagede), Bali handicrafts.
- Bali's dry season (Apr–Oct) is peak, especially Jul–Aug and Christmas–New Year. Nov–Mar is wet but cheaper. Jakarta floods are a risk in Jan–Feb. Bandung is cooler (~18–25°C).
- Don't drink tap water. Pack meds for "Bali belly".
