# Nordic Europe 🇫🇮🇳🇴🇸🇪🇩🇰🇮🇸 (FI · NO · SE · DK · IS)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- **Schengen, visa-free:** 90 days in any 180, counted across the whole Schengen area together, not per country. All five countries are in Schengen. Norway and Iceland are not in the EU. Passport must be valid 3+ months after you leave Schengen.
- **EES (Entry/Exit System)** has been fully live since 10 Apr 2026 and replaces passport stamps. At your first entry you give fingerprints (age 12+) and a face photo at a kiosk or counter. Expect long queues and leave extra time for connections.
- **ETIAS** is not live yet. It was planned for Q4 2026, the EU has dropped that target, and launch is now expected in 2027 with several months' notice (verify on travel-europe.europa.eu). The fee will be €20 (free under 18 and over 70). Use only the official site; lookalike sites charge extra.
- **UK ETA** (needed if your route goes via London): Malaysians need one, £20 since 8 Apr 2026, apply on the UK ETA app. An airside connection at Heathrow or Manchester that doesn't pass UK border control is currently exempt, but the exemption is temporary (verify). Separate tickets, or changing airports, mean you need an ETA.

| IS | ISK | ~0.033 (Aug 2026) | ISK 12,000 per receipt |

- **Very expensive.** A casual main in Oslo is about NOK 200–380 (RM86–165). Rovaniemi hotels in December averaged ~€350 a night in 2023 (≈RM1,600), and peak nights cost more. Lapland Christmas sells out up to a year ahead.
- **Near-cashless:** pay by card or Apple/Google Pay almost everywhere, including toilets and parking. Some Swedish shops don't take cash at all. Bring a Wise or BigPay card and maybe €50 in cash.
- **Save money:** self-cater from discount supermarkets (Lidl, Rema 1000, Kiwi, Netto, Bónus), eat the weekday set lunch (*lounas*, *dagens rett*, *dagens lunsj*) and book apartments with kitchens. Tap water is excellent.
- **Tax refund:** have the form stamped by customs at your final EU/Schengen exit before check-in, and don't use the goods first. Norway and Iceland have their own schemes. Global Blue and Planet run the refunds.

## Plug & power
Type C/F, 230V. Malaysian Type G plugs need an adapter, but no voltage converter.

## Getting around
- **Trains:** VR (FI), Vy (NO), SJ (SE), DSB (DK). Book Lapland night trains and the Oslo–Bergen line early. Iceland has no trains.
- **Driving:** on the right. Carry a JPJ International Driving Permit with your MY licence (verify each country; Norway accepts non-EEA licences for 3 months). Winter tyres are compulsory in Finland and Sweden in winter, and required in Norway when conditions call for them. Rental cars normally come with them. In Iceland, F-roads are closed in winter, check road.is and safetravel.is daily, and gravel insurance doesn't cover F-roads.
- **Data:** a Malaysian roaming pass, or a Europe eSIM that lists Norway and Iceland (both are outside the EU). Coverage on main roads is good.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Sundays:** Norway closes most shops (petrol stations and small grocers open). DK, SE and FI shops mostly open with shorter hours.
- **Christmas:** **24 Dec is the main day.** Shops close at noon (DK) or by 16:00 (NO), and many restaurants close 24–26 Dec. 1 Jan is quiet too. Easter (Thu–Mon in NO/DK) and Midsummer Eve (25 Jun 2027, SE/FI) empty the cities.
- **July:** many locals are on holiday, and some city restaurants close for 2–4 weeks.
- **Aurora:** the season runs from about Sept to early April, best 20:00–02:00 on clear nights. Book tours with a free-rebook policy.
- **Polar night:** Tromsø has no sunrise from about 27 Nov to 15 Jan, with a few hours of blue twilight at midday. Rovaniemi only gets about 2 sunless days. Lapland is often −15 to −30°C. Pack thermal base layers, wool, down, insulated boots and spare phone batteries.

## Must-eat (diet notes)
| Dish | Diet note |
|---|---|
| Salmon / gravlax, shrimp smørrebrød | No pork; some smørrebrød use pork (*leverpostej*, *flæsk*) |
| Reindeer (Lapland), lamb soup (IS) | Not halal unless stated |
| Cinnamon buns (*kanelbulle*/*korvapuusti*) | Usually fine; check gelatine in some pastries |
| Fish & chips, fish soup | Safest halal-friendly default |
| Hot dogs (DK/IS *pylsur*) | Usually contain pork or lamb, so skip |

Halal: easy in Oslo (Grønland), Stockholm (Kista, Rinkeby), Copenhagen (Nørrebro) and Helsinki (verify specific spots). Limited in Reykjavík. Hard in Tromsø and Rovaniemi, so self-cater with fish or bring sealed food. Prayer times are extreme in winter and summer, so use a high-latitude setting in prayer apps.
