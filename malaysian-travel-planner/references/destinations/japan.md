# Japan 🇯🇵 (JP · JPY)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. Items marked (verify) must be re-checked before you state them.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- Short visits are visa-free with an e-passport (up to 90 days; verify on the MOFA Japan visa-exemption page).
- **Visit Japan Web**: fill it in before departure to get the immigration and customs QR codes.
- Japan plans an electronic travel authorisation (JESTA) around 2028. If the trip is close to that date, check.

## Money
- Cash is still needed at small restaurants, temples, markets and shrines. 7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs take foreign cards.
- IC transport card: Suica / PASMO (Tokyo) or ICOCA (Kansai), usable nationwide on trains and in konbini.
- **Tax-free shopping changed on 1 Nov 2026:** you now pay the 10% tax at the till and get a refund after departure. Keep your passport and receipts. Ask the shop how the refund is paid (card, app, etc.). Don't use consumables in Japan if you want the refund. Thresholds: check the Japan Tourism Agency page.
- Multi-currency cards (Wise, BigPay, etc.) avoid high bank FX fees.

## Plug & power
Type A (two flat pins), 100V. Malaysian Type G plugs need an adapter. Phone and laptop chargers are fine; 240V-only hair dryers and straighteners are not.

## Getting around
- The JR Pass got much pricier in 2023 and often isn't worth it for Osaka–Kyoto–Nara loops. Compare it with single tickets and regional passes (Kansai, Hokkaido, Kyushu).
- Use luggage forwarding (takkyubin) between hotels for shopping hauls. Coin lockers are at most stations.
- Apps: Google Maps (works well), Japan Transit / Jorudan, GO or Uber (taxi).

## Closed days & timing traps
- Many **museums close Monday** (Tuesday if Monday is a public holiday).
- **29 Dec–3 Jan**: many attractions, museums and small restaurants are closed.
- Small restaurants often close 1–2 weekdays a week; last order is 30–60 min before closing. Check Google Maps or Tabelog for each place.
- Kyoto **Nishiki Market**: many shops close Wednesday and/or Sunday. Osaka **Kuromon**: some stalls close Sunday.
- Popular spots (USJ, teamLab, Shibuya Sky, Ghibli Museum, Pokémon Café) need **advance tickets**.

## Must-eat (diet notes)
| Dish | Where | Diet note |
|---|---|---|
| Sushi / kaisen-don | Everywhere; Tsukiji / Kuromon markets | No pork; rice vinegar and mirin are not halal-certified |
| Ramen | Tonkotsu = pork broth; shoyu and shio usually chicken/pork | Look for halal ramen shops in Tokyo and Osaka |
| Takoyaki / okonomiyaki | Osaka | Okonomiyaki often has pork; choose seafood |
| Wagyu yakiniku / sukiyaki | Everywhere | Halal-certified wagyu restaurants exist in Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto |
| Kushikatsu | Osaka Shinsekai | Some skewers are pork |
| Hitsumabushi (eel) | Nagoya | No pork |
| Soup curry, crab, jingisukan (lamb BBQ) | Hokkaido | Lamb is not halal-certified unless stated |
| Matcha sweets | Kyoto / Uji | Usually fine for vegetarians |

Halal resources: JNTO Muslim-friendly guide, Halal Navi app. Prayer rooms are at major airports and some department stores.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Drugstores, Pokémon Center, Loft, and Japan-only Uniqlo or GU collabs are the "worth it" shopping (see brands-in-malaysia.md).
- Sushiro, Ippudo, Yoshinoya and DON DON DONKI exist in MY. Lower their priority.
- Winter (Dec–Feb) is often below 5°C and Hokkaido is well below 0°C. Pack heat-tech and gloves.
