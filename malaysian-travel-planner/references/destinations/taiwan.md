# Taiwan 🇹🇼 (TW · TWD)

*Last reviewed 2026-11. (verify) = re-check before stating.*

## Entry for Malaysian passport holders
- Visa-free short stay for Malaysians (up to 30 days under the visa-waiver programme; it is renewed periodically, so verify).
- **TWAC (Taiwan Arrival Card)** is online only since Oct 2025. Submit it in the days before arrival (window reported as up to 7 days; verify). It's free. One person can file for a group.

## Money
- Cash is still common (night markets, small eateries). Cards are fine in malls and chains.
- **EasyCard** for MRT, buses and convenience stores.
- Tax refund (TRS): spend NT$2,000+ at one participating store on the same day. Small amounts may be refunded on the spot.

## Plug & power
Type A/B, **110V**. Phone and laptop chargers are fine. Malaysian 240V-only hair dryers and kettles will not work properly.

## Getting around
Taipei MRT, HSR (Taipei ↔ Kaohsiung ~1.5h), TRA trains to Hualien and Yilan. Uber and taxis are easy.

## Closed days & timing traps
- **Lunar New Year**: many family-run shops close for the first few days.
- Many restaurants close one weekday (often Monday). Check Google Maps for each.
- **Typhoon season Jul–Sep** can cancel flights and Taroko/east-coast trips.
- National Palace Museum: check its opening days on the official site.

## Must-eat (diet notes)
| Dish | Diet note |
|---|---|
| Beef noodle soup | Beef, but broth is not halal |
| Xiaolongbao (Din Tai Fung original) | Usually pork; DTF also exists in MY |
| Lu rou fan, oyster omelette, gua bao | Pork / seafood |
| Taiwanese fried chicken, bubble tea, mango shaved ice | Chicken not halal unless certified |
| Pineapple cake, nougat | Souvenirs |
| Vegetarian buffets (素食) | **Great for no-pork and vegetarian travellers**: Buddhist vegetarian food is everywhere |

Halal: limited but growing. The Chinese Muslim Association certifies halal and Muslim-friendly restaurants, and there are halal options around Taipei Grand Mosque.

## Malaysian-specific tips
- Chatime, Tiger Sugar, Xing Fu Tang and Din Tai Fung all exist in MY. Prioritise night-market stalls and local-only shops instead.
- Mandarin speakers will find Taiwan very easy.
- Winter (Dec–Feb) is about 12–18°C and wet in Taipei. Bring a light jacket.
